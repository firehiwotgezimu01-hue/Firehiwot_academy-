Grade 9–12 Physics: Motion, Forces and Energy

1. Motion

Motion is a change in the position of an object over time.

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Example: A student travels 100 metres in 20 seconds.

Speed = 100 ÷ 20 = 5 m/s.

2. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity over time.

Acceleration = (Final velocity − Initial velocity) ÷ Time

Its SI unit is metres per second squared (m/s²).

3. Newton's Second Law

The net force on an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration.

F = m × a

Force is measured in newtons (N), mass in kilograms (kg), and acceleration in m/s².

Example: If m = 2 kg and a = 3 m/s², then F = 6 N.

4. Work and Energy

Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance in the direction of that force.

Work = Force × Distance

Kinetic energy is the energy of motion.

Kinetic Energy = ½ × mass × velocity²

5. Electricity

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

Ohm's Law: V = I × R

Voltage (V) = Current (I) × Resistance (R).

Quick Review

  1. What is the SI unit of force?
  2. A car travels 150 m in 10 s. Calculate its average speed.
  3. Calculate the force on a 4 kg object accelerating at 2 m/s².
  4. Write the formula for Ohm's Law.

Answers: 1. Newton (N). 2. 15 m/s. 3. 8 N. 4. V = I × R.
